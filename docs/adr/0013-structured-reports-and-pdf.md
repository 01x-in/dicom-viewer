# ADR 0013: Structured Reports are read by the engine into plain measurements, and the PDF report is written by a small writer of our own

Status: accepted (M10). Builds on [ADR 0004](0004-viewer-state-and-ordering.md) (one queue), [ADR 0005](0005-engine-runtime-lifecycle.md) (give back what the engine took), [ADR 0008](0008-measurements.md) (measurements are data in the core), and [ADR 0010](0010-shell-polish.md) (panel controls follow the tokens and keep their `data-*` names). The plan is in [docs/specs/sr-import.md](../specs/sr-import.md).

## Context

A person asked whether the viewer reads GSPS or Structured Reports. It read neither: an SR series was listed and then skipped. M10 reads the measurements of a TID 1500 measurement report, and adds a PDF report of the measurements on screen.

## Decision

- **A report becomes measurements, and nothing else.** `viewer.measurements.loadSr(seriesInstanceUID)` keeps the shapes of the report as ordinary measurements with the origin `import`. The viewer's six types are kept (length, bidirectional, ellipse, rectangle, probe, angle). A shape of another type (circle, arrow, free-hand outline, Cobb angle) is counted, not kept. The numbers stored in the report are not shown: the viewer works length, angle, area, and Hounsfield statistics out again from the points and the pixels, so a number on screen always matches the image.
- **Left out is counted, by reason.** `SrLoadResult` is `{ added, replaced, skipped: { reason, count }[] }`. The reasons are: an image that is not in the study, a shape the model rejects, a group the reader could not read, a kind of shape the viewer has no place for (with the names), and an id that a measurement made some other way already holds (a report never replaces a measurement a person or the host made). A report that adds nothing says why. `importJson` is all or nothing because a file the viewer wrote should be perfect; a report comes from outside, so a partial load is normal.
- **Ids are stable.** A measurement made from a report has the id `sr:<SOP instance UID of the SR>:<position in the report>`, so loading the same report again replaces its measurements. Skipped shapes keep their position, so the ids of the others do not move.
- **The engine reads, the core maps.** [engine/sr.ts](../../packages/core/src/engine/sr.ts) is the only code that touches `@cornerstonejs/adapters` for reports (ADR 0003 and the architecture rule). It returns plain shapes: a type, points in patient millimetres, a label, and the SOP instance each is on. [measurements/sr.ts](../../packages/core/src/measurements/sr.ts) turns them into measurements through `parseMeasurement` and counts what it cannot. The registry fetches the report and the series it names, and runs in the queue.
- **Three things the Cornerstone reader needed.**
  - It looks for a shape inside the numeric item of a measurement group. The standard's template, and tools that follow it (highdicom), put the shape beside the number, and the reader fails on such a group with only a log line. The engine moves a shape that sits beside a number into it before it calls the reader.
  - It catches an error inside a group and goes on, so a failed group is simply missing. The engine counts the groups itself and reports the difference as unreadable.
  - It writes the longer axis of an ellipse first and returns it that way, while the model wants the vertical axis first. The engine puts the axes in the model's order, with the direction of the image's rows.
- **Images are held only while the report is read.** The reader turns pixel positions into millimetres with what Cornerstone knows about each referenced image. The engine registers the metadata of the instances nobody holds, claims them, reads, and releases them in a `finally`, as it does for a SEG. The matrix checks that the count of held images is the same after a load as before.
- **`dcmjs` is a dev dependency of core**, exact-pinned (0.52.0) like the Cornerstone packages. The reader needs a dataset made with it. It was already inside the engine chunk through the adapters.
- **The PDF is made in the browser by a writer of our own.** [report/pdf.ts](../../packages/core/src/report/pdf.ts) writes A4 pages with the two standard fonts every PDF reader has, and lays text out from the fonts' own widths. It is about 5 kB and its own lazy chunk (2 kB brotli), loaded only when a report is made. [measurements/report.ts](../../packages/core/src/measurements/report.ts) builds the report as plain data first, so its rows, order, and the patient switch are tested as data.
- **Patient identity is off by default.** The report names the patient only when the viewer's overlay is set to `showPatientInfo`, the same switch the overlay has. With it off, the name, the ID, and the birth date are not in the report model at all. The study and series descriptions are shown. A footer on every page says "Software for building viewers. Not for diagnosis."
- **Text the font cannot draw is shown as `?`, and the page says so.** The standard fonts hold Latin-1. ASCII, the degree sign, the square, the middle dot, and the accented letters of Latin-1 are drawn. Any other character (for example Chinese) is drawn as `?`, and the page says how many were. A label is free text, so the writer never fails on it and never drops text without saying.
- **The panel and a command.** The measurements panel has a "Load report" select that lists the SR series of the study (it is absent when there are none) and says what was added and left out, and a "Download PDF" button, disabled with no measurements. The button runs the command `measurements.downloadPdf`, which a config file can put on the toolbar as data. A series of modality `SR` is listed and not selectable, as a SEG is.

## Evidence

- **The PDF library was measured, not guessed.** An empty document bundled with esbuild and compressed with brotli: jsPDF 2.5.2 is about 99 kB, pdf-lib 1.17.1 about 164 kB. The writer of our own is 2 kB. The report needs one table with page breaks and a footer, which is much less than either library does.
- **A report from a tool other than Cornerstone is read.** `fixtures/phantom/sr-measurements` is written by highdicom, with six groups on four slices. The `structured report` scenario of `pnpm matrix --only headless` loads it in Chromium, in both asset modes, and checks: five shapes added and the circle counted as left out; the length 27 mm, the angle 90°, the probe in the water insert 0 HU, the ellipse in the bone insert 1000 HU, the rectangle in the fat insert −100 HU, and the areas, all to 0.05; the names are the findings of the report; loading again replaces and does not add; no image is held afterwards; the PDF is a PDF, names the shapes, says `Imported`, and does not name the patient. Reports written by Cornerstone's writer (all six types) round-trip to 0.01 mm in a unit test.
- **The PDF reads in a PDF reader.** A sample with 45 rows over five pages, a label that wraps, and one with Chinese characters was read by poppler (`pdftotext`, `pdftoppm`). The unit tests check the file's offset table, the page count, and the footer on every page.
- **Size.** The engine chunk is 801 kB brotli against its budget of 880 kB. The viewer chunk is 24.0 kB against 26 kB. A new budget line holds the PDF chunk at 3 kB.
- The shell scenario, with axe, still passes with the new controls, with a report available.

## Consequences

- **Only TID 1500 measurement reports are read.** A report of another template, text only, or with no image coordinates is refused or empty. GSPS is not read.
- **Only what Cornerstone's reader understands.** Shapes are recognised by the tracking identifier the Cornerstone tools write (`Cornerstone3DTools@^0.1.0:Length` and so on). Another tool's report is read only when its identifiers and shapes follow that. highdicom's, written in that way, is. A report from a vendor has not been tried, and the viewer does not claim it works.
- **Single-frame images only.** A shape on a frame of a multi-frame image is looked up as frame 1.
- **A bidirectional measurement is not in the committed fixture.** highdicom's measurement groups hold one shape, and a bidirectional measurement has two. It is tested with Cornerstone's writer at test time.
- **The report is Latin-1.** A name in another script shows as `?`. Embedding a font would fix it, at the size of the font. The writer does not do that.
- **No version number in the report.** The build has none to read.
