# ADR 0009: SEG overlays are read on the compute worker, kept as data in the core, and drawn as Cornerstone labelmaps

Status: accepted (M6). Builds on [ADR 0004](0004-viewer-state-and-ordering.md) (one queue for every engine call), [ADR 0005](0005-engine-runtime-lifecycle.md) (give back what the engine took), and [ADR 0008](0008-measurements.md) (data in the core, a copy in the engine).

## Context

M6 adds DICOM SEG overlays: a SEG is loaded against the series it names, each of its segments can be shown, hidden, recoloured, made more or less opaque, highlighted, and gone to, and the same state must show in every viewport. The plan sets a budget: 512 by 512 by 300 pixels with 100 segments in under 3 seconds, with the segment index built on the worker.

The M1 spike loaded a SEG through Cornerstone's adapters and showed nothing. Measured on a generated SEG of that size (143 MB, 4500 frames), the adapters took 3.8 s on the page's main thread, which misses the budget and freezes the page while it runs. The index on the compute worker took 0.3 s.

## Decision

- **A loaded SEG is plain data the viewer owns.** `viewer.segmentations` has `list`, `get`, `load`, `unload`, `setVisible`, `setSegmentVisible`, `setSegmentColor`, `setSegmentOpacity`, `setOpacity`, `highlight`, and `jumpToSegment`, and the events `segmentation:loaded`, `:unloaded`, `:updated`, `:visibility`, and `:warning`. Every call goes through the viewer's queue. [registry.ts](../../packages/core/src/segmentations/registry.ts) is the one place that changes a SEG. A `SegmentationInfo` holds the SEG's series, the series and stack it is drawn on, its opacity and visibility, its highlight, its warnings, and for each segment its number, label, colour, opacity, visibility, pixel count, image count, and centre in patient millimetres.
- **What is drawn is worked out in the core.** [display.ts](../../packages/core/src/segmentations/display.ts) turns those settings into a colour, an opacity, a visibility, and an outline flag for each segment: the opacity of the segment times that of the SEG, a hidden SEG shows nothing whatever its segments say, a highlight in `dim-others` mode takes the others to a fifth of their opacity, and a highlight in `outline` mode outlines the chosen segments and leaves every fill alone. A highlight never shows a segment that is hidden. The engine only applies the result (`StackPort.drawSegmentation`), so the rules are unit tested without an engine.
- **The reconciler is the registry.** It knows every stack on screen (`attach`), draws a SEG on each stack that shows the series and stack it names, and draws it again on every change. A viewport that comes later draws what is loaded, as it looks now. Showing, hiding, colour, and opacity are therefore the same in every viewport by construction, and the matrix checks it with two viewports of one series.
- **A SEG belongs to one study.** Loading another study unloads every SEG and gives its memory back; `destroy()` does too. A host that wants one to survive loads it again.
- **The header is read from the bytes, by the engine, without Cornerstone.** [segmentation-header.ts](../../packages/core/src/engine/segmentation-header.ts) takes the segments, the instances and planes each frame names, the geometry, the type, and where the pixel data is, with dicom-parser, and checks it with Zod. It is tested on the real phantom files. It finds the series and the stack a SEG names (the stack that holds most of the instances it lists); a SEG whose images are not the size of its own is refused, because it cannot be drawn on them.
- **A binary SEG is decoded on the compute worker.** The adapters read a SEG with dcmjs on the main thread. [decode-binary.ts](../../packages/core/src/segmentations/decode-binary.ts) goes from the bit-packed pixel data and the frame list to labelmap layers and the index of every segment in one pass, skipping empty bytes, and a segment that overlaps an earlier one goes on a layer of its own. Frames are placed on images by the instance they name ([plan.ts](../../packages/core/src/segmentations/plan.ts)). Cornerstone's compute worker `expose`s its methods once, so the worker build gives it a wrapper around comlink that adds ours ([comlink-expose.ts](../../packages/core/workers/comlink-expose.ts)): there is still one compute worker and no new file to ship in `url` mode. The same function runs on the page if the worker cannot be had. A SEG that is not binary, or whose frames do not all say which segment and which instance they are, goes through the adapters as before.
- **The adapters are given what they look for.** Cornerstone v5's `generalImageModule` no longer has the SOP instance UID, which the SEG adapters read there, so every frame fell back to matching by position, with a warning for each frame, and a SEG whose planes are off found none. The adapters are given a metadata provider that adds it.
- **Each layer is a Cornerstone segmentation.** Colours go in the representation's colour table with the opacity as its alpha, and visibility goes through `setSegmentIndexVisibility`; only what changed is sent. Cornerstone outlines all the segments of a labelmap or none, so outlining some takes a second segmentation over the same labelmap images, drawn as outlines only, with the segments that are not highlighted hidden. Labelmap images are made for every image of the stack, empty ones too: where there is none, Cornerstone goes on drawing the labelmap of the last image that had one (the matrix found this on a SEG that covers 48 of 64 images). Hiding, disposing, and destroying remove the representations, the colour tables, the segmentations, the images, and the style entries; `getEngineDiagnostics()` counts the segmentations, and the 20-cycle leak check loads and outlines a SEG in each cycle and fails if one is left.
- **Geometry is checked, and a doubt is a warning.** [validate.ts](../../packages/core/src/segmentations/validate.ts) compares the SEG with the images it names and says (`segmentation:warning`, and in `SegmentationInfo.warnings`): another frame of reference, another direction, other pixel spacing, frames whose plane is off the plane of their image (more than 0.1 mm), instances the series on screen does not have, segments that share pixels, segments with no pixels, and (`not-drawn`) a SEG the engine would not draw. A SEG with warnings is still drawn, on the images it names. The overlay does not use the SEG's own planes, so a SEG that is off the CT is drawn where its references put it, and the warning is the only thing that says so.
- **Jumping.** `jumpToSegment(series, segment, { strategy, viewportId })` shows an image of the segment in the series the SEG names, in one queued step like a jump to a measurement. `largestSlice` (the default) is the image with most of its pixels, the first when several tie. `centroid` is the image of the segment nearest its middle (the segment's middle can fall between two lumps, so the nearest image that has the segment, not the one at the middle). `nearestTo` is the image of the segment nearest the image on screen. A segment with no pixels rejects.
- **`navigateTo` takes SEGs.** `segmentations: [{ seriesInstanceUID, visible, highlightSegments? }]` loads the ones that are not loaded, sets them, and highlights. It runs after the slice is on screen, because loading is slow and the image must not wait. A SEG that cannot be loaded does not stop the navigation or the others: it is in `failedSegmentations` of the result and reported as an `error` with the context `segmentation`. One not listed is left alone. Segment numbers and UIDs are checked before anything is queued.
- **The shell.** A built-in `segmentations` panel is the second tab of the right-hand dock: `<dv-segmentation-list>`. It lists every SEG of the study with Load, and for a loaded one a checkbox and an opacity slider for the whole of it, Unload, its warnings, and for each segment a checkbox, a colour input, an opacity slider, Highlight, and Go to. Sliders send the newest value when the viewer has answered the last. `hide-segmentations-panel` removes it; React and Vue have `hideSegmentationsPanel` and `useSegmentations`.
- **Not in `ViewerStateV1`.** A link should not carry what is loaded or how it looks, for the reason measurements are not in it. The controlled `state` prop stays deferred to M7.
- **The M1 spike is gone.** `viewport.loadSegmentation` and the port's `SegmentInfo` are removed.

## Evidence

The `segmentation` scenario of `pnpm matrix --only headless` reads the viewport's canvas, against the real engine and the phantom, in both asset modes:

- every colour is what it should be to within 8 of 255: the body and the marker in the colour of segment 1 at half strength, the bone insert in segment 2's, outside the body untouched; the bone insert in blue, at opacity 0 (as the plain CT), at full strength, with the body hidden, the SEG hidden, and everything restored;
- a highlight in `dim-others` mode takes the body to a tenth (half of a fifth) and leaves the bone insert; in `outline` mode at least 6 of 12 pixels round the bone insert change, its inside and the body's fill do not;
- `largestSlice`, `centroid`, and `nearestTo` go to images 8, 31, 55, and 8 as worked out from the phantom, and the viewport shows them; the body's largest image is the first;
- the four inserts are drawn on images 8 to 55 and not on image 2;
- `navigateTo` with a SEG shows image 20, loads it, and highlights segment 4;
- the mismatch SEG has the warnings `frame-of-reference` and `plane-position` ("21 frames of the SEG are not on the plane of the image they name, by up to 12.0 mm") and is drawn on the images it names; the overlap SEG has two layers, the warning, and both colours show where they meet; the fractional SEG goes through the adapters and comes out the same;
- two viewports of one series follow one call, and one attached later draws what is loaded as it now looks;
- after unloading, no segmentation is held.

The `segmentation budget` scenario generates the SEG of the plan (512 by 512 by 300, 100 segments, 143 MB, [perf.py](../../tools/phantom/perf.py), not committed) and loads it: 4,447,300 pixels in 100 segments in about 2.1 s on the development machine, the request for the file from a server on the same machine included, against 3 s. The decode alone is 1.2 s (the adapters take 3.8 s). CI runs the same scenario with a limit of 9 s, because a shared runner is several times slower than that machine.

The `lifecycle` scenario (20 mount and unmount cycles) loads a SEG in each cycle, outlines a segment, and unloads it in every other one, leaving the rest to `destroy()`: nothing is left in the engine after any cycle. The `shell` scenario walks the Segments tab in every framework example (load, hide a segment from the keyboard, recolour, change opacity, highlight, go to, load the mismatch SEG and read its warnings, unload), with axe run with the tab empty, with a SEG loaded, with warnings showing, and in the light theme.

## Consequences

- **Memory.** A layer holds a labelmap image for every image of the stack: 78 MB for 512 by 512 by 300, for each layer. While a SEG loads, the file, its copy on the worker, and the decoded layers are alive together, about three times the file.
- **Only binary SEGs whose frames name their instances take the fast path.** Fractional SEGs, and any SEG that does not say which instance a frame is on, are read by the adapters on the main thread, at the adapters' speed. SEGs of the newer labelmap type have not been tried.
- **The viewer's own reader does not look at direction.** It places each frame on the image it names, so a SEG that lies in another direction than its images is drawn on them, with a `plane-orientation` warning. The adapters refuse an oblique SEG, so one that goes through them (not binary, or frames that do not name their instances) cannot be loaded.
- **Where two layers overlap, Cornerstone shares the overlay equally** between them (a quarter of the image shows, and each colour counts for three eighths), rather than one covering the other.
- **Rendering needs WebGL.** Cornerstone draws a labelmap as an image actor and fails on a stack rendered on the CPU (`cpuRendering`). The engine refuses to draw then, and the SEG says so with a `not-drawn` warning, which was checked in the real engine: it loads, its numbers are right, and nothing is drawn.
- **Highlight has no settings.** The outline is 3 pixels and a dimmed segment keeps a fifth of its opacity.
- **No creating, editing, or exporting a SEG, no statistics of a segment, and nothing in MPR** (M7 owns SEG in MPR).
- **One stack per series for measurements is unchanged**, so a page with two viewports of one series should not also draw measurements on both.
